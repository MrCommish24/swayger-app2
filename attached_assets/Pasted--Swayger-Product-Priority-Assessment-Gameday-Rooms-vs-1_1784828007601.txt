# Background

Swayger was originally built around 1v1 sports challenges.

The basic 1v1 concept is:

* One person creates a sports prediction or challenge
* Another person accepts it
* Each person locks in their take
* The result is resolved
* The app creates a record or “receipt” showing who was right
* Users can build a history, record, reputation, or standing over time

The issue is that the 1v1 product has a meaningful cold-start problem.

A user must:

1. Understand the product
2. Decide to create a challenge
3. Identify someone to challenge
4. Send the challenge
5. Get the other person to open it
6. Get the other person to accept
7. Wait for the relevant sports result
8. Return to resolve or view the result

We have found it difficult to consistently convert new users into completed 1v1 challenges.

Swayger Gameday Rooms appear to have more traction.

The Gameday experience allows multiple people to join a shared room around a live sporting event and make picks during several stages of the game.

The Gameday experience currently includes or has included:

* Shared Gameday Rooms
* Join-by-link functionality
* Pregame picks
* Halftime picks
* Fourth-quarter or clutch picks
* Pick lock times
* Ability to update picks before locking
* Host-managed room experience
* Host resolution of picks
* Live or updated leaderboard
* Final standings
* Shareable post-game results or receipts
* Private group participation
* Multiple participants in the same room
* Game-specific props
* Repeat engagement throughout the same sporting event

The intended Gameday flow is generally:

1. A room is created for a sporting event
2. A link is distributed
3. Participants join the room
4. Participants make pregame picks
5. Picks lock
6. Halftime picks open
7. Halftime picks lock
8. Fourth-quarter or clutch picks open
9. Final results are resolved
10. Participants see standings and shareable results

During previous testing, Gameday Rooms produced more participation and repeat engagement than standalone 1v1 challenges.

The current application, however, may still be structured and presented primarily as a 1v1 product, with Gameday Rooms discoverable somewhere within the larger Swayger experience.

We want to understand what it would take to reposition the application so that:

> Swayger Gameday becomes the primary user experience and acquisition wedge, while 1v1 challenges remain available as a secondary feature.

This does not necessarily mean deleting or rebuilding the 1v1 system.

The possible product hierarchy could become:

* Gameday Rooms as the primary home experience
* My Picks or My Rooms as a primary destination
* Leaderboards and results as a primary destination
* 1v1 challenges as a secondary feature
* 1v1 challenges possibly initiated from inside or after a Gameday Room
